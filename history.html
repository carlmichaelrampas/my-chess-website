A very short history of chess

Chess has been around for roughly 1,500 years and has changed hands, rules and names along the way. Here is the whole story in a few minutes.

Origins: India, Persia, Europe

Chess most likely began in India around the 6th century as chaturanga — a game played on an eight-by-eight board with pieces representing the four divisions of an army: infantry, cavalry, elephants and chariotry. That is why the pieces still feel like an army.

It travelled to Persia, where it became shatranj, and the Persian word shah (king) is the ancestor of the word "check" and of "chess" itself. When the game reached the Islamic world it was studied seriously, and from there it spread across Europe by around the year 1000, largely through Spain and Italy.

The modern rules

For centuries the queen moved only one square at a time and the bishop was a much weaker piece. In 15th-century Europe both were given their long-range powers, which made the game far more dynamic — contemporaries called it "mad queen chess". Those changes, together with castling and the modern pawn's two-square first move, produced essentially the game played today.

Chess today

Chess is now played by hundreds of millions of people, online and over the board. Programs are far stronger than any human, so the interesting questions have shifted: how do players use engines to prepare, and how do they keep the game creative?

Gukesh Dommaraju has been world champion since December 2024 and defends the title against Javokhir Sindarov in late 2026. Magnus Carlsen, champion from 2013 to 2023, remains the highest-rated classical player, and Ju Wenjun of China is the reigning Women's World Champion.

Why the history matters Knowing where an opening came from — and which ideas survived a century of testing — is often a faster route to understanding it than memorising the moves.
